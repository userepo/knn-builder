All 58 builds completed. They read the vectors from host memory, as the service builds at ea7db9f, so the device never holds the dataset whole: the largest, 2M vectors of dimension 384 at m 16, peaked at 626 MiB, where with the dataset on the device it took 3,556 MiB. The device peak model is the larger of an IVF-PQ phase (the PQ codes, a batch of at most 65,536 vectors in their own type, and buffers of about 530 KB a dimension) and a graph phase (the finished graph and a buffer over at most 500,000 rows). The batch is measured, not fitted: where a float build and an fp16 one of the same shape both peak in the IVF-PQ phase, as 13 of the 24 pairs do, they peak exactly 65,536 rows of d values times the 2 bytes between them apart, 48 MiB at d 384 and 96 MiB at d 768; the others peak in the graph phase, one or both, and differ by less or not at all. The model is within 0.8% on every build, 0.6% on the builds left out, and its worst under-estimate is 0.8%; the codes, the batch and the graph are floors it only adds to, so it never forecasts less than they take, past the grid included.

Reading the free memory in a tight loop found the 5 ms samples' peaks on the five builds `check.json` repeats, and on 34 of the 36 builds of 250k vectors or fewer (`tight.json`). The other two, 100k vectors of dimension 128 at m 16, finish their IVF-PQ phase between two samples: 100 MiB against the 68 sampled (float), 76 against 52 (byte). The fit and knn-builder's tests take a build's peak as the larger of its two readings. The held-memory builds prove nothing on this host: WSL2's driver pages device memory out to the host instead of refusing it, so the builds leaving 90% of the peak free completed as well.

`first.json` covers what the grids leave out. Every grid build follows a warm-up build in its process, and a process's first build takes more: cuVS loads its kernels and creates its library handles then, and keeps them. Six builds, from 1,000 vectors of dimension 64 to 1M of dimension 768, each peaked 40 to 42 MiB higher as its process's first build than after a warm-up, whatever its size, so the memory knn-builder wants free for a build is the modeled peak, the 6% margin and 42 MiB, since the first job of a service is such a build. The same file has builds of fewer vectors than the batch's 65,536: 1,000 float vectors and 1,000 fp16 ones peak alike at 16 MiB, where a batch of 65,536 rows would set them 8 MiB apart, so the batch holds no more vectors than the job has. Below the grid's 100,000 vectors the model over-estimates: 10,000 and 20,000 vectors of dimension 128 peak at 40 MiB, against 70 and 75 MiB modeled.

The build time is off by 11% at the median and 30% at worst on the builds left out; d 768 at m 32 builds two to five times slower than at m 16 (80.0 s against 15.4 s at 1M vectors), as the IVF-PQ search's lookup table and top-k no longer fit a block's shared memory. Download and upload times carry LocalStack's own spread. The index file's size is exact. The CPU model is within 13% on the sizes it was fitted to and under by 14 to 23% at 500k vectors, where HNSW slows faster than n log n. On 4 threads a 500k x 768 build took 62 s. The service's part of the same remote build took 16 s, but the data node's wait is longer: at these transfer rates it uploads the vectors in about 4 s, sees the job complete at its second poll, about 20 s in (the first comes about 15 s after the request, then one every 5 s), and downloads the file in about 5 s, about 29 s in all.

All 24 byte builds completed, and the models hold for them with one change. The device peak model, its batch a byte a value, is within 0.8% of every byte build, 0.25% at the median and at worst 0.8% under, as on the float builds it was fitted to; a byte build's peak is 76 to 100% of the float build's of the same shape, the batch the only part that shrinks. A byte build takes about as long as a float one, 0.78 to 1.08 times on this grid, so the build time counts a byte value as 4 bytes, not 1: it is then off by 8% at the median and 36% at worst, against 12% and 38% on the float builds it was fitted to. Download and upload times are within 14% and 21%, the file size within 0.01%. Its model is off by 5% at the median and 13% at worst on the builds left out. The device peak is the dataset and the larger of two phases: NN-descent's, which holds a second copy of the dataset and its graph and peaks only at 2M vectors of 1,024 bits at m 16, and the graph's optimization, the graph and a buffer of about 4.1 bytes a neighbor over at most 520,000 rows, as for float. The service builds a binary request through NN-descent with Hamming distance (`GpuIndexBinaryCagra`), m kept as requested, into the ID map over `IndexBinaryHNSWCagra` over `IndexBinaryFlat` (`IBMp` > `IBHc` > `IBxF`), or a `null` storage with `skip_stored_vectors`, which its patched Faiss passes through for binary too. Recall@10 is 1.0 on every committed input, 0.878 to 0.895 over the 20 builds of the uniform 20,000-vector input, and 1.0 on all of the clustered one's, whose 400 clusters leave no neighbor to miss. With 40 vectors cuVS lowers both degrees to 39 (logging it), the file's M is then 19, a level 0 of 38 slots that takes the first 38 of each vector's 39 neighbors, and the five builds are identical; at 150 the degree stays 64. Binary files record no numeric type, and `keep_max_size_level0` 0, where float ones record 1. Per `sizes.json`, NN-descent costs time where IVF-PQ costs little: 1M vectors of 1,024 bits build in 55 s at a peak of 626 MiB, of 256 bits in 14 s at 534 MiB.

Builds are not reproducible: the five files of one input share their headers and level tables but differ in their graphs, so `knn-gpu` is held to the recall spread, and `knn-write` to the oracle, which rebuilds each file under `service/`, `byte/service/` and `binary/service/` exactly from its own graph.

The oracle rebuilds every byte file `knn-write` wrote from its own graph exactly.

Over the uniform 20,000-vector binary input, `knn-gpu`'s mean recall is the service's, 0.8880 against 0.8883, a fifth of a standard error apart; 4 of its 20 builds fall just outside the service's 20 (0.877 and 0.897, against 0.878 to 0.895), as about 2 would for two samples of one spread. The clustered input recalls 1.0 for both, and every binary file `knn-write` wrote is the oracle's. On the 40-vector input four of the five builds are the service's file exactly, the degree lowered to 39 as the service's was; the first pass's differs.

## At ea7db9f

`ea7db9f/` holds the service's builds at ea7db9f, its `api-snapshot` image pinned in `tools/README.md`, with `project_link_to_base_layer` left off, as k-NN leaves it, everywhere but under `projection/` (described last): `baseline-small.json`, `byte-baseline-small.json` and `binary-baseline-small.json` hold five builds of every committed input, as under `service/`, `byte/service/` and `binary/service/`; `baseline-large.json` holds five builds of each 20,000-vector float input, as `service/baseline-large.json`; and `service-20000x128.json`, `service-byte-20000x128.json` and `service-binary-20000x256.json` hold 20 builds of each large input, as their namesakes under `parity/`; `burst-upstream.json`, `bench-upstream/` and `bench-knn-builder/` hold the burst and benchmark runs, the last two with `gpu.csv`, the benchmark's `nvidia-smi` samples (local time, MiB used), and the `knn-gpu-*` files and `consecutive/` knn-builder's parity builds, all described below. The files under `service/`, `byte/service/` and `binary/service/` stay those of 3a02e64, which the oracle in ea7db9f's `faiss-base` image rebuilds exactly as before.

At ea7db9f, upstream's patch 0003 builds a float, fp16 or byte graph from the vectors in host memory, where at 3a02e64 it first copied them to the device; binary builds are unchanged. The graphs did not change measurably. Every file is the size it was at 3a02e64, and the oracle rebuilds each of the 210 exactly from its own graph. Every recall mean is within 1.6 standard errors of 3a02e64's; over 20 builds, the means are 0.76890 against 0.76945 (float L2), 0.77295 against 0.77340 (float inner product), 0.77575 against 0.77500 (byte L2), 0.77445 against 0.77455 (byte inner product) and 0.89055 against 0.88830 (uniform binary), with 6 of the 100 builds just outside 3a02e64's range, as about 10 would for two samples of one spread. The build step takes about as long, its median over 20 builds 0.35 and 0.34 s against 0.385 and 0.395 s on the two 20,000-vector float inputs, and the binary 40-vector builds are again all one file, the committed one.

Two builds at once still crash the service under WSL2. `ea7db9f/burst-upstream.json` is `burst/upstream.json`'s burst again, 48 requests to the service on its default two workers, and it ended the same way: the process exited (139) as its first two builds started, every data node's status request failed, and all 48 segments went to the CPU.

The device memory the build saves shows on the README's benchmark. `ea7db9f/bench-upstream/` is `tools/bench/run.py`'s single builds of `cohere-l2-1000000x768` on the upstream service at ea7db9f, three of them (`--phases single --runs 3`), with `run.json` and `recall.json` as under `bench/upstream/`: each peaked at 0.87 GB of device memory, where `bench/upstream/` has 3.74 GB at 3a02e64, 2.87 GB less: the 3.07 GB of vectors no longer copied to the device, less the 65,536 of them (192 MiB) the IVF-PQ build holds there at once. The build step took 18.7 s at the median against 19.9 s, and the first file recalls 0.968 against 0.966. `tools/bench/report.py` prints its row from these files.

`ea7db9f/bench-knn-builder/` is the same three builds by knn-builder's image building from host memory as well: each peaked at 0.82 GB, where `bench/knn-builder/` has 3.70 GB through the device copy, the same 2.87 GB less, in a build step of 16.8 s against 15.0 s, its first file recalling 0.964 against 0.969. The grid reads the same build at 0.82 GB (`grid/runs.json`), and one such build sampled by `nvidia-smi` and by the device's own free memory at once read 786 MiB by both.

The `gpu.csv` files show what the benchmark's device peaks are measured from. The idle knn-builder process holds 127 MiB from its start (its CUDA context and resources, which it makes before any job) and 169 to 171 MiB after a build (what cuVS keeps); the upstream process holds nothing until its first build and 129 to 142 MiB after. `nvidia-smi` under WSL2 reads 0 MiB used once the GPU has idled for about ten seconds, whatever a process holds, passing through one to three other values a sample each on the way down and back up, so the least seen during a job is no measure of what its process held: `tools/bench/run.py` takes the level the samples last stayed at, two samples or more, since the process started. The runs under `bench/` and `ea7db9f/` took the least seen and so read 127 to 171 MiB high on 40 of knn-builder's 91 builds under `bench/` and 56 of the upstream's; their `run.json` files carry the peaks and the levels held (`device_held_bytes`) recomputed by the new measure from the samples the runs kept, committed here as `gpu.csv` for the runs under `ea7db9f/` only.

`knn-gpu-20000x128.json`, `knn-gpu-byte-20000x128.json`, `knn-gpu-small.json` and `knn-gpu-byte-small.json` are `knn-gpu`'s builds of the same inputs from host memory, as the service builds at ea7db9f, by its `build_inputs` example (20 and 5 builds). Over 20 builds the recall means are 0.77325 against the service's 0.77295 (float inner product), 0.76980 against 0.76890 (float L2) and 0.77395 against 0.77445 (byte inner product), every committed input recalls 1.0 on both, and the oracle rebuilds every file `knn-write` wrote here and in `consecutive/`, 210 of them, exactly from its own graph.

Byte L2 came out 0.77410 against 0.77575, three standard errors apart, and `consecutive/` shows why. A build's recall depends on its place in its process's sequence of builds, in a cycle of four, while the files all differ: 40 builds of that input in a row recall about 0.783, 0.777, 0.752 and 0.780 in turn. `consecutive/` holds two such runs for each service, each from a fresh process. Both services cycle alike, each one's first run from the same values (0.789, 0.778, 0.752) and its second within 0.003 of them, so the cycle is cuVS's, and over their 80 builds each they recall 0.77350 (the service) and 0.77359 (`knn-gpu`), every `knn-gpu` build within the service's range; at each place in the cycle the two differ by at most 0.0020, either way. Where two inputs alternate, as in the 20-build runs, each samples only some places of the cycle, and which ones depends on all the process built before, so two processes can differ by a few thousandths on one input though their builds do not. Consecutive builds of one input from a fresh process compare the two fairly.

Two of the service's answers changed with its FastAPI update (0.120.1 to 0.162.0, with Starlette 1.10): a build request with no `Content-Type` header, or an empty one, is refused with 422 as not an object, where it was read as JSON; and a body of JSON `null` is refused the same way, where it was taken as no body.
